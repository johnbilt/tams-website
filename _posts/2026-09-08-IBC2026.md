## Talks

### Friday 11th

#### 15:00:  Stop Copying Media: TAMS for Open, AI-Ready Media Workflows (Content Everywhere Stage 2)

Why does every workflow still need its own copy of the media? In this session, Oracle Cloud Infrastructure and Norsk explore how TAMS enables a more open model: store media once, make it accessible across workflows, and give broadcasters more choice in the tools and partners they use. The result is less duplication, faster access, and more flexibility across clipping, replay, publishing, archive, analytics, and emerging AI-enabled media operations. Rather than locking media into closed silos, TAMS points to a practical cloud foundation for simpler operations, greater customer choice, and faster innovation. Discover why time-addressable media is emerging as a smarter foundation for the next generation of media workflows.

### Monday 14th

#### 12:00:  Open Media Architectures (AWS - 5.C90)

The media industry is shifting from monolithic, proprietary systems toward open, modular architectures that decouple content storage, processing, and distribution into interoperable layers. This approach enables organisations to compose best-of-breed tools, accelerate innovation, and integrate AI natively across production workflows — from ingest through to delivery. 

This is being driven by open-source initiatives including TAMS (Time Addressable Media Store) which reimagines cloud storage as a real-time media platform, and MXL (Media eXchange Layer) providing standardised transport between media services.  Together they illustrate how open architectures reduce vendor lock-in, lower integration costs, and empower media organisations to evolve at the pace of technology rather than procurement cycles.

With John Biltcliffe (AWS), Chris Swan (AWS) and Sam Mesterton-Gibbons (BBC R&D)

## Demonstrations

### AWS (5.C90)

This year, rather than a standalone TAMS demo pod, TAMS serves as a foundational layer underpinning several demonstrations across the AWS booth — reflecting its growing role as core infrastructure for modern media workflows. In Re-inventing Linear Contribution & Distribution on AWS, TAMS powers both ingest and fast-turnaround workflows feeding directly into linear channel playout, demonstrating how cloud-native media storage can streamline traditional broadcast chains. The Adopting MXL for Live Cloud Production demo showcases TAMS playback and recording as part of a wider, gallery-based live production environment built on the MXL standard, illustrating how TAMS and MXL work together as complementary technologies for cloud-based production. And in Smart Stories: The Agentic Production Ecosystem, developed through the IBC Accelerator programme, TAMS acts as the media store alongside the SOM bus as the story context layer, enabling next-generation news workflows that include AI-driven understanding of TAMS content, AI-assisted story writing, and automated publishing to multiple endpoints.

Beyond the demos, TAMS also features in the AWS booth theatre programme, with a session from Yahoo on Saturday and an Open Media Architectures session on Monday lunchtime exploring the broader ecosystem around TAMS. Visitors looking to get hands-on will also find Logic Media and Merapar on the AWS booth — both systems integrators with live TAMS demonstrations and real-world experience building with TAMS, ready to help customers looking to adopt it in their own environments.

[https://aws.amazon.com/events/ibc-2026/](https://aws.amazon.com/events/ibc-2026/)

### Zixi (5.A76) - New

"ZEN Master, powered by Broadcaster v19, writes live streams directly into a TAMS (Time Addressable Media Store) as time-stamped segments that can be reached at any point on the timeline. Any Zixi input, including SRT, RIST, Zixi protocol, and HLS, becomes content addressed by time rather than by file. Teams can work with the stream while it is still being written, take only the interval they need, and build clips or edits by reference with no rendering and no file transfers. Broadcaster handles protocol reliability, timing, and packaging ahead of the store, so TAMS receives a clean, continuously timestamped record of the event. 

The store becomes a landing point for the feeds Zixi already carries. A venue contributing over SRT or Zixi protocol lands in TAMS within seconds, so a producer can pull a replay or highlight by timestamp while the event is on air. A RIST hand-off from a partner network becomes a time-indexed record for compliance, rights review, or late-joining editors. An HLS source can be normalized by Broadcaster and stored once, then re-cut for social, syndication, or on-demand without a second transcode.The live demo shows this end to end: pick a TAMS environment and flow, watch segments appear as Broadcaster writes them, or query the store by time range, then play the results in the browser or download them stitched into a single clip."

[https://www.zixi.com](https://www.zixi.com)
